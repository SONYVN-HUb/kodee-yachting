Five hours of sunset: an afternoon on a sailing catamaran in Phu Quoc

8 min read · Updated 20/11/2026

There's a kind of silence that only shows up at sea in the afternoon. Not total silence — there's still water lapping at the two hulls, the rigging humming softly, someone laughing at the bow — but the kind that makes you naturally lower your voice. This post is about five hours like that: a Sunset Charter in Phu Quoc, the slot Theia Yachting runs from 15:00 to 20:00.

We're not describing any one person's trip. We've rebuilt it from what the boat really has and what the package really includes, so when you book, you know exactly what you're stepping into.

15:00 — When the car pulls up at the pier

The afternoon starts in your hotel lobby, not at the pier. On the 1360 – Coral II sailing catamaran, round-trip hotel transfers are already in the package. It's a small detail that changes your whole mood: no maps, no parking, no showing up early “just in case”. Just come down to the lobby in your sandals with a light bag.

Coral II is a 13.6-metre catamaran, 6.9 metres wide, built in 2021. That 6.9-metre beam is the first thing you feel when you step on board: the deck is surprisingly wide for its length. The twin hulls keep the boat steady on the water, and with up to 28 guests, your group — a family of ten or a crew of twenty friends — still has room for everyone to find their own corner.

15:30 — A welcome drink and a map you don't need to open

People often imagine a yacht trip as a schedule planned to the minute. In reality, the best thing about a private charter is that you don't share your schedule with anyone. Theia describes Coral II's route as cruising along the island's southern waters and visiting nearby islets — where you stop and for how long depends on the wind, the water and your group.

Welcome drinks come out as the boat leaves the pier. Fresh seasonal fruit waits on the shaded outdoor dining table. Ask anyone who's done a lot of boat trips and they'll tell you: spend the first ten minutes just sitting still. Don't rush for photos. Let your eyes get used to the colour of the water.

The ultimate luxury on a private yacht isn't the best seat — it's that nobody tells you to get up.

16:30 — Into the water

When the boat anchors in a calm bay, the afternoon shifts gear. Coral II's package includes snorkeling gear, SUP boards, fishing gear and an inflatable water slide. The kids (and plenty of adults) go for the slide first. The quiet ones stand up on a SUP and paddle slowly around the hull. The most patient person drops a line off the stern and declares the first fish theirs.

Storyteller's tip: get in the water at least once, even if you're not planning to swim. Sit on the edge of the hull, dangle your feet in the water and look up at the boat from below — it's an angle very few photos capture, but you'll remember it.

17:30 — Dinner as the light changes colour

Food and drinks on board are included. This is when Coral II's outdoor dining area shines: a table long enough for the whole group, with shade for anyone who's had enough sun for the day. You'll notice people talk differently at sea than on land — slower, with fewer glances at their phones.

If your group has a special occasion — a birthday, a wedding anniversary — this is the right moment to bring out the cake. (Let us know before booking so we can confirm the arrangements with Theia.)

18:00 — A sunset that needs no commentary

We won't describe the sunset. Every travel article tries and fails in the same way. Just one practical point: the 15:00–20:00 slot is designed so you're on the water exactly as the sun goes down. The exact anchoring spot is up to the captain based on the day's weather — and that's a call you should trust.

Tip for photo lovers: the bow nets are the best spot for backlit shots, but put the camera away for the last five minutes. Some moments are better kept in your eyes than on your phone.

19:30 — Fireworks and the ride home

One detail few people notice in Coral II's service list: “Fireworks (evening tour)”. When it's fully dark and the lights on shore come on, this is the finale many groups choose for their afternoon. The transfer car is waiting at the pier to take you back to your hotel. Twenty minutes later you're in your room, hair still smelling of sea salt, and the afternoon has become a story.

Quick summary: Sunset trip on Coral II
  • Sunset Charter slot: 15:00–20:00 (5 hours)
  • Yacht: 13.6 m sailing catamaran, up to 28 guests, Phu Quoc
